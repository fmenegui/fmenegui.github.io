Hospital integration · InCor · 2023–2026

ECG classification at InCor

Heart Institute (InCor), Hospital das Clínicas, University of São Paulo Medical School

Objective

The aim was to help the emergency team prioritize ECG review. The classifier presents its analysis in the application clinicians already use at the hospital.

My work

I developed the models and worked on deployment in the emergency department. I also coordinated annotation of around 20,000 ECGs with 19 cardiologists, organizing class definitions, review of disagreements and agreement analysis. After deployment, I worked on cardiologist review and comparison with specialists.

Design choices

Within the hospital system
We use ECGs stored in PACS and show classifications in the emergency application alongside medical review.
Annotation with cardiologists
I organized annotation around class definitions, review of conflicting readings and agreement analysis between cardiologists.
Model versions
The 2023 version uses three classes. The 2026 research evaluates 12 categories. That test result describes the model studied, not every deployed version.

How it works

In the architecture we published in 2023, the 12-lead ECG is stored in DICOM format in the hospital’s picture archiving and communication system (PACS). The AI module retrieves the examination, prepares the image and classifies it with a convolutional neural network. The figure also shows age, sex and ethnicity as inputs.

Results and evaluation

The 2023 version classified examinations as atrial fibrillation, other or normal. We later worked with a ConvNeXt ensemble for 12 categories. In the 2026 study of 18,519 ECGs from 14,402 patients, held-out macro F1 was 0.807 (95% CI: 0.788–0.825).

Deployment was reviewed by a cardiologist and compared with specialists. The figures above describe the research dataset and test results, rather than the number of examinations processed in routine care.
