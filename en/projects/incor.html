Hospital integration · InCor · 2023–2026

ECG classification at InCor

Heart Institute (InCor), Hospital das Clínicas, University of São Paulo Medical School

Objective

We built the classifier to help emergency department clinicians decide which ECGs to review first. Its predictions appear in the hospital application they already use.

My work

I developed the models and helped deploy the classifier in the emergency department. I coordinated the cardiologists' ECG annotations, including class definitions, review of conflicting readings and agreement analysis. After deployment, I helped evaluate the system with a cardiologist and compare it with specialists.

How it works

In the architecture published in 2023, 12-lead ECGs are stored in DICOM format in the hospital's picture archiving and communication system (PACS). The AI module retrieves the ECG, prepares the image and classifies it with a convolutional neural network. The figure also shows age, sex and ethnicity as inputs.

StageApproach
Recording and storage12-lead ECGs stored in DICOM format in PACS.
ProcessingImage preprocessing and classification with a CNN.
OutputAtrial fibrillation, other or normal.
Clinical usePredictions shown in the emergency department web app to help clinicians prioritize ECG review.

Results and evaluation

The 2023 version classified ECGs as atrial fibrillation, other or normal. We later developed a ConvNeXt ensemble for 12 categories. In the 2026 study of 18,519 ECGs from 14,402 patients, it achieved a macro F1 of 0.807 on the held-out test set (95% CI: 0.788–0.825).

Evaluation after deployment included cardiologist review and comparison with specialists. The figures above describe the research dataset and its held-out test results.
