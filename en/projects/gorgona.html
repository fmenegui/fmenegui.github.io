Gorgona

ECG classification integrated into urgent care

Heart Institute (InCor), Hospital das Clínicas, University of São Paulo Medical School

Objective

Gorgona analyzes ECGs recorded at urgent care units and notifies the clinical coordination team when an ECG suggests current of injury. The alert arrives through Telegram while the examination also goes to telemedicine. Clinicians assess the patient and decide on care.

My work

I led the project and developed the initial integration of the model with the Windows application, InCor server, dashboard and Telegram. I worked with the clinical team, who defined the care protocol and reviewed the ECGs.

Design choices

Telemedicine
We kept the telemedicine workflow and added automated alerts. Clinicians remain responsible for assessment and care decisions.
Where the model runs
The Windows application watches the examination folder and queues uploads. Classification runs on the InCor server. Local controls let staff restart the application or resubmit files.
Data protection
The client offers configurable local anonymization. Uploads use HTTPS and credentials for each installation. These features need to be considered within the data protection requirements.

Deployment location

The deployment shown here was at UPA Rodrigo Argolo, an urgent care unit in Tancredo Neves, Salvador. ECGs are recorded at the unit, and the model runs on the InCor server.

How it works

Previous workflow

Staff sent the ECG to telemedicine manually. The report and medical assessment informed whether to contact the coordination group on Telegram. If clinical suspicion was high, the physician could also contact the group directly.

With Gorgona

We added automatic submission: the application detects the file in the examination folder and sends it to InCor for classification. If the probability of the Current of Injury class exceeds 50%, the system alerts the configured group.

Windows client, server and online platform

The application at the unit

The ECG-IA Client watches a folder on Windows and queues new examinations for upload. Each installation has its own credential. The system tray menu lets staff configure the application, restart it or resubmit files from the folder.

Server and online platform

On the server, a FastAPI endpoint receives the examination, reads its metadata and converts the trace to an image when needed. We store classes and probabilities in SQLite. The dashboard shows results and client status, and the Telegram integration sends the alerts.

Data protection and LGPD

The client has an option to anonymize files on the unit’s computer before upload. Transmission uses HTTPS, with credentials for each installation and user access roles. These are technical data protection controls and do not, by themselves, demonstrate full compliance with Brazil’s LGPD.

The dashboard filters results by unit, period, status and class.

Results and evaluation

Case study

In the encounter documented on May 20, 2026, at UPA Rodrigo Argolo, admission was at 18:52. Two ECGs were recorded, and each triggered an alert 2 minutes later. Arrival at the referral center was recorded at 20:44.

The first alert arrived 4 minutes before the examination reached telemedicine. The alerts preceded the reports by 41 and 25 minutes. These times show that information reached the coordination team earlier. This single case does not establish that AI improved treatment times or outcomes.

Classification model

We started from the AIMED model. I led the team in the 2024 PhysioNet Challenge, where we placed first in classification. The CinC 2024 paper describes ConvNeXt, ECG pretraining and the ensemble. My thesis details the image datasets, experiments and clinical evaluation.

The classifier uses a ConvNeXt ensemble for 12 categories. In the study of 18,519 ECGs from 14,402 patients, annotated by 19 cardiologists, held-out macro F1 was 0.807 (95% CI: 0.788–0.825). This is the model’s evaluation result in that study.
